Why the app exists

Left to ourselves, we tend the garden badly.

Interleaf decides for you not because you can't learn — but because decades of evidence show that good scheduling runs against our instincts. When learners are handed the controls, they reliably make the same wrong calls.

What we actually do

We reach for cramming and rereading — the strategies that fade fastest.

Surveys of real students find that last-minute cramming and rereading dominate, even though they produce some of the least durable learning. We keep choosing them because, in the moment, they feel productive.

Hartwig & Dunlosky, 2012 Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell, 2013
The illusion

A leaf that looks green isn't a healthy root.

When material feels fluent while you study, it feels like learning — but that fluency barely predicts what you'll actually remember later. Mistaking ease for mastery is one of the most robust findings in the field.

Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell, 2013
The preference

We pick the worse method even right after watching it lose.

When practice is mixed, people learn more yet believe they learned less — so they choose the blocked practice that works worse, moments after the mixed version outperformed it in front of them.

Kornell & Bjork, 2008
The trap

The more alike our skills look, the worse we choose.

When categories resemble one another, learners lean even harder into studying them one at a time — exactly the situation where mixing would have helped most.

Tauber et al., 2020

This is the gap Interleaf fills. It isn't that you can't tend your garden — it's that good scheduling fights your instincts every single session. So the app makes those calls, and you just tend what it hands you.

How it works

One score decides which plant to water.

Every skill is scored, every session, on four things. The one that needs you most wins — with a little patience built in, so the recommendation doesn't flit around mid-session.

score = α · thirst − β · fatigue + γ · readiness − λ · crowding
💧

Thirst

How close a skill is to being forgotten — measured from real graded recall, never a hopeful self-rating (which research shows runs overconfident).

🍃

Fatigue

Four separate beds — logical, verbal, visual, fine-motor — so moving from Python to guitar can rest one part of your mind while another works.

☀️

Readiness

A skill that needs an unmet prerequisite is kept out of the bed entirely — you're never sent to tend something that can't grow yet.

The part most study apps won't tell you

The famous “interleaving effect” doesn't apply across a garden. We say so.

Novel — being tested, not assumed

The interleaving effect you've read about — mixing practice to sharpen the difference between similar things — needs confusable material (Brunmair & Richter, 2019). Guitar, Spanish, and Python aren't confusable, so we don't claim that effect for cross-domain switching — and you should doubt anyone who does.

What Interleaf actually claims across unrelated skills is narrower and honest: forced spacing, helpful variety, and resting different parts of your attention. Every session is logged precisely so this can be measured on real learners — not asserted and left to grow wild.
